## 1. A-198 — the Israeli VAT year

`src/lib/goldenYearFixtures.ts` → `GOLDEN_IL_YEAR`, `verifyIlGoldenYear()`, `goldenIlYearTotals()`.

Twelve months of 2026 documents for one VAT registration (`vat:IL:514000000`, bi-monthly). Every
amount is a whole agora, and the report figures for each period are **literals in the fixture**;
`verifyIlGoldenYear()` runs `israelVatReturn` (AI #1's library, unchanged) over the same documents
and compares all eight figures.

| Period | Taxable sales | Output VAT | Zero-rated | Exempt | Input — equipment | Input — other | To pay / (reclaim) | Documents |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Jan–Feb | 15,000 | 2,700 | 20,000 | — | — | 720 | 1,980 | 5 |
| Mar–Apr | 7,000 | 1,260 | — | — | — | 360 | 900 | 3 |
| May–Jun | 12,000 | 2,160 | 4,000 | — | 3,600 | 900 | **(2,340)** | 4 |
| Jul–Aug | 12,000 | 2,160 | — | — | — | 450 | 1,710 | 3 |
| Sep–Oct | 15,000 | 2,700 | — | 2,000 | — | 720 | 1,980 | 3 |
| Nov–Dec | 17,000 | 3,060 | — | — | — | 900 | 2,160 | 4 |
| **Year** | **78,000** | **14,040** | **24,000** | **2,000** | **3,600** | **4,050** | **6,390** | **22** |

(Shekels; the fixture stores agorot — ₪15,000 = 1,500,000.)

Checked by hand:

- **Rate:** 2026 is 18% all year (`ilVatRates`, asserted from 2025-01-01). ₪10,000 + ₪5,000 =
  ₪2,700 output in Jan–Feb.
- **Zero-rated vs exempt:** the export (₪20,000) and the Eilat sale (₪4,000) sit in the zero-rated
  box, never the taxable one; the ₪2,000 exempt sale has its own box. The three never mix.
- **Credit note:** the April credit (net ₪1,000, VAT ₪180) reduces **March–April**, the period it
  belongs to: taxable ₪8,000 − ₪1,000 = ₪7,000 and output VAT ₪1,440 − ₪180 = ₪1,260 (126,000
  agorot, the figure the test asserts).
- **Equipment apart:** the ₪20,000 equipment bill's ₪3,600 input VAT is reported in the equipment
  box, not mixed into other inputs; the period is a refund of ₪2,340.
- **Year identity:** output − (equipment + other input) = the sum of the six periods' net
  (1,404,000 − 765,000 = 639,000 agorot). The test recomputes the whole year as one return and
  asserts both.

## 2. A-199 — the Texas sales-tax year

`GOLDEN_TX_YEAR`, `verifyTxGoldenYear()`, `goldenTxYearTotals()`.

A Texas permit (`sales_tax:US-TX:32000000000`) selling through 2026, reported quarterly, with one
resale-certificate customer, one out-of-state (Oregon) buyer and one credit note. **State base rate
only — 6.25%** (`usStateSalesTax`); no county, city or transit rate is in the scenario, and the
screen says so.

| Quarter | Gross | Exempt | Taxable | Tax collected | Documents |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Q1 | 16,000.00 | 2,000.00 | 14,000.00 | 875.00 | 3 |
| Q2 | 9,000.00 | — | 9,000.00 | 562.50 | 3 |
| Q3 | 28,333.33 | 5,000.00 | 23,333.33 | 1,458.33 | 3 |
| Q4 | 18,595.67 | — | 18,595.67 | 1,162.23 | 2 |
| **Year** | **71,929.00** | **7,000.00** | **64,929.00** | **4,058.06** | **11** |

Checked by hand:

- **Out of state:** the Oregon sale is not on the Texas return at all (the buyer's state decides);
  it is not counted as exempt either.
- **Certificate:** the resale customer's sales are exempt (the certificate is good on the date), not
  "missing tax".
- **Credit note:** Q2 is 750,000 + 250,000 − 100,000 = 900,000 cents taxable.
- **Rounding:** 333,333 × 6.25% = 20,833.3125 → 20,833; 1,234,567 × 6.25% = 77,160.4375 → 77,160;
  625,000 × 6.25% = 39,062.50 → 39,063 (half up), giving Q4 = 116,223.
- **Internal identity:** every quarter's gross = taxable + exempt (asserted for each quarter through
  `usSalesTaxReturn` itself), and the four quarters' tax sums to the year total.
- **Jurisdiction split:** the only part is "Texas state sales tax" — the fixture proves the state
  share alone, which is exactly what the screen's limit says.

## 3. A-106 — the 1099 thresholds by year

`src/lib/tax/us1099Thresholds.ts` — `US_1099_THRESHOLDS`, `threshold1099CentsFor`,
`is1099Reportable`, `us1099ThresholdProblems`.

| Form and box | Threshold | Payments from | Through |
|---|---:|---|---|
| 1099-NEC box 1 | $600 | 2020-01-01 | 2025-12-31 |
| 1099-MISC boxes 1 and 3 | $600 | 2020-01-01 | 2025-12-31 |
| 1099-NEC box 1 | $2,000 | 2026-01-01 | in force |
| 1099-MISC boxes 1 and 3 | $2,000 | 2026-01-01 | in force |
| 1099-MISC box 2 (royalties) | $10 | 2020-01-01 | in force |
| 1099-MISC box 10 (attorney) | $600 | 2020-01-01 | in force |

- The 2026 change is the 2025 tax act's: the ordinary threshold is $2,000 for **payments made after
  31 December 2025**. The rows are keyed by the calendar year of the payment.
- **One answer:** a test compares every row with E-10's existing `threshold1099Cents` for each year
  2020–2030; the two cannot drift.
- A year no row covers throws rather than returning a guessed amount; `us1099ThresholdProblems()`
  fails the test suite if a gap appears between 2020 and 2027.
- A reference table only: exemptions, the payee's status and the instructions in force govern a
  filing; nothing files.

## 5. Limits and hand-offs

1. **A-198 proves the maths, not the product path for equipment.** `israelVatReturn` is fed
   `fixedAsset: true` on a bill in the fixture. Real bills need a persisted classification
   (issue #160's adapter) before the equipment box can be produced from a workspace's own bills;
   that is AI #1's lane.
2. **A-199 is state-level.** Local rates are not in the fixture, and the screen says so; the state
   return screens' own wiring (CON-1/CON-2) remains with the lead.
3. **Do not turn a fixture into a filing.** Nothing here posts, files, pays or closes, and the
   screen repeats that.
4. **A-107 (GSA per diem) and A-102 (Bituach Leumi)** were deliberately not taken: this sandbox has
   no general internet, and a rate that cannot be checked against the authority's page is not
   written.
